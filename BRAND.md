# Brand Book

Companion to `RESEARCH.md` (product/competitive) and `MARKET.md` (sizing/economics).
This document covers positioning, naming, verbal and visual identity, and store presence.

Research date: July 2026.

> **Not verified:** name candidates below have **not** been trademark-screened or
> domain-checked. I can reason about collision risk from category knowledge, but I have not
> run USPTO Class 9/42 searches or live domain lookups. Treat §3 as a shortlist to screen,
> never as cleared names. §3.4 is the screening protocol.

---

## 1. Strategy

### Positioning statement

> For the parent who runs a household's food, **[Name]** is the shared shopping list that
> learns your store and shows what the trip costs — unlike AnyList and Bring!, it has no ads,
> no brands buying placement, and nothing to sell but the app itself.

### Who it's for

From `MARKET.md`: a **35–54 parent coordinating a household**. Families with children are 168%
more likely to shop online; 35–54s are 41% of monthly online grocery shoppers; a family of four
spends ~$920/month on groceries. They are time-poor, price-aware, and coordinating 2–4 people.

Not for: Gen Z (high adoption, low spend, low willingness to pay), or 55+ (12% online grocery
adoption). Both may use it. Neither shapes the paywall or the copy.

### Three pillars

Everything the brand says traces to one of these. If it doesn't, cut it.

| Pillar | Claim | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| **Knows your store** | The list reorders itself into your aisle order | Learned from check-off sequence; per-store profiles |
| **Shows the damage** | You know what the trip costs before the register | Seed estimates → your own price book |
| **Nobody's product but yours** | No ads, no sponsored items, no data sold | Free tier is genuinely free; subscription is the whole model |

### Personality

Placed on axes rather than adjectives, because "friendly" is unfalsifiable:

```
casual    ·······●··  formal          → plainspoken, not chatty
playful   ·····●····  serious         → dry warmth; a joke is fine, a mascot is not
warm      ··●·······  clinical        → this is someone's family food
direct    ·●········  suggestive      → say the thing
expert    ······●···  peer            → competent friend, not an authority
```

The reference point is a **capable friend who shops a lot** — not a nutritionist, not a
butler, not a robot, and emphatically not a cartoon vegetable.

### Anti-positioning

Explicit, because these are the gravitational pulls in this category:

- **Not a deals app.** No coupons, circulars, or "save 30% on Tide." That's Bring!'s and
  Listonic's business, and it makes brands the customer.
- **Not a wellness app.** No calories, macros, scores, or nudges about what you eat. Judging a
  shopping list is the fastest way to lose a household.
- **Not a productivity tool.** No projects, assignees, due dates, workspaces, or roles.
  Trello and monday.com solve a different problem; borrowing their UI would be a category error.
- **Not cute.** No mascot, no anthropomorphized groceries, no confetti-for-everything. One
  moment of delight on completing a list is the entire budget.

## 2. Competitive brand audit

### Verbal territory (from their own store copy)

| App | Their line | Territory held |
|---|---|---|
| AnyList | "The #1 app for busy families… shop smarter and simplify weekly meal planning" | **Busy families + meal planning.** Broad, confident, feature-forward |
| Listonic | "The best grocery list – free, simple, and available on all devices" | **Free and simple.** Volume play, price-led |
| Bring! | Lovingly drawn item tiles; "create a list in seconds" | **Visual delight + speed** |
| OurGroceries | "Simple, effective, no frills" | **Reliability.** Almost anti-brand |

**Unclaimed:** nobody owns *cost*. Not one of the four leads with what the trip will cost you,
despite households spending $5,750–6,240/yr on groceries and wasting $1,500–2,900/yr in food.
Nobody owns *store intelligence* either — aisle sorting exists but is sold as a feature, never
as the reason the app exists. And "no ads" is currently a footnote, not a flag, even though
Listonic's banner-heavy free tier has created live search demand for alternatives.

### Visual territory

The category is overwhelmingly **green** — Instacart, Woolworths, Amazon Fresh, and most
grocery apps default to it. Green is so saturated here that it reads as generic; it signals
"grocery" and nothing else. The rest split between clinical white/grey list UI (AnyList,
OurGroceries) and illustration-led tiles (Bring!).

**The opening:** warm, papery, adult, and *not* green. Warmth is the household register the
clinical apps miss; restraint is what Bring!'s illustration style gives up.

## 3. Naming

### 3.1 Criteria

From 2026 naming and ASO research:

- **≤7 characters.** The average top-100 app name is 7 characters or fewer. Both stores cap
  the name at 30 characters including spaces, which the ASO title (brand + 1–2 keywords) eats.
- **A real word used unexpectedly** — the dominant 2026 naming trend, and it aids recall.
- Trivially pronounceable and spellable on first hearing, since word of mouth between household
  members is the growth loop.
- Not descriptive-generic ("Grocery List Pro") — unregistrable, and invisible in search.
- Room to mean more than groceries later, without meaning nothing now.

### 3.2 Shortlist

| Name | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Errand** (6) | The job-to-be-done, named plainly. Adult and warm without being cute — matches a 35–54 parent, not a teen. Nobody in the category is near it. Extends past groceries (hardware, pharmacy) without diluting today. | Not food-evocative alone; leans on the ASO subtitle to say "grocery" |
| **Tally** (5) | Doubles as *check off* and *running total* — literally both differentiators in one word. Short, warm, plain. | **High collision.** A well-known US fintech uses it, plus several utilities. Likely blocked in Class 9 |
| **Bushel** (6) | Distinctive, warm, agricultural; sounds like abundance and provisioning. Low consumer-app density. | An agtech/farm-management company uses it — probable Class 9/42 conflict |
| **Sundry** (6) | Literally "miscellaneous small items"; unexpected real word, quietly clever. | Slightly obscure; some will misread as "Sunday" |
| **Aisle** (5) | On the nose for the store-intelligence pillar. | Very high collision; heavily used in retail-tech and consumer brands |

### 3.3 Recommendation

**Errand**, with **Sundry** as the fallback.

It names what the user is actually doing rather than what the software is, which is why it
still works when the app grows past groceries. It's warm without being twee, it survives being
said out loud across a kitchen, and it sits in a part of the category nobody occupies — every
competitor is named after the *object* (list, groceries, basket, cart), which is exactly why
those names are crowded and unregistrable.

ASO title: **`Errand: Shared Grocery List`** — 27 characters, brand plus the two head terms.

**Tally** is the better *idea* and I'd want it if it were free; I don't believe it is, and a
name you can't defend is worse than the second-best name.

### 3.4 Screening protocol — do this before any spend

1. **USPTO search in Class 9 (downloadable apps) *and* Class 42 (SaaS)** — search both;
   category guidance is explicit that software marks live in both. Include **phonetic** and
   near-miss variants, not just exact strings, since phonetic matches draw refusals.
2. **Common-law check** — an unregistered mark in active commercial use can still block you.
3. **Store search** on both App Store and Google Play for existing apps using the name.
4. **Domains** — `.com` and `.app` at minimum; verify before committing, so a competitor
   can't sit on the obvious one.
5. **Social handles** across the platforms you'll actually use.
6. Only then: file Class 9, and Class 42 if a web app ships.

Budget for this to eliminate your favorite. Screen all five in parallel, not sequentially.

## 4. Verbal identity

### Voice principles

Specific and prescriptive, because "friendly and approachable" instructs nobody.

1. **Say the thing.** "Milk is $0.40 more than last month," not "We noticed a change in your
   spending patterns."
2. **Never judge the cart.** No praise, no concern, no commentary on what a household eats.
   Not "great healthy choices!" — not anything.
3. **Admit what you don't know.** "We're guessing ~$4" beats a confident wrong number. This is
   the estimated-price rule as a *voice* rule, and it's the brand's whole credibility.
4. **Household, not team.** "Maria added this." Never "assigned to," "owner," or "member."
5. **Short enough to read one-handed, holding a bag.**

### Lexicon

**Use:** trip · household · aisle · your store · added · got it · usually · roughly ·
last time · price book · estimate

**Ban:** users · consumers · SKU · optimize · leverage · seamless · journey · task · assign ·
deals · savings · unlock · premium experience · AI-powered · smart (as a prefix for anything)

"Smart" is banned specifically because every competitor uses it and it has stopped carrying
information.

### Before / after

| Situation | ✗ | ✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Empty list | "No items yet! Tap + to get started 🎉" | "Nothing on the list." |
| Estimated total | "Total: $28.00" | "≈ $28 · 3 estimated" |
| Item not recognized | "Error: item not found in database" | "Added. Tap to file it under an aisle." |
| Invite | "Invite team members to collaborate" | "Shopping with someone? Send them the list." |
| Offline | "Connection lost. Retrying…" | "Offline — everything's saved, it'll sync." |
| Paywall | "Unlock premium features!" | "The list is free forever. Price history is $19.99/yr." |
| Sync conflict | "Conflict detected in list state" | "Ariel added milk too — kept one." |

### Taglines

- **"Know what the trip costs."** — leads with the unclaimed territory. Recommended.
- "Your store, in order." — leads with aisle intelligence.
- "The list that pays attention."
- "No ads. Ever. Just the list." — sharpest against Listonic; weakest as a standalone brand line
  because it's defined by an opponent.

## 5. Visual identity

### Color

Deliberately **not green**. The category is saturated with it to the point of invisibility, and
warmth is the register the clinical list apps miss. Built on 60-30-10: 60% neutral surfaces,
30% supporting, 10% accent.

| Role | Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper | `--bg` | `#F6F4F1` | App background. Warm off-white, not clinical grey |
| Card | `--card` | `#FFFFFF` | Item rows, sheets |
| Ink | `--ink` | `#1B1A18` | Primary text. Warm near-black, never `#000` |
| Muted | `--muted` | `#8D8880` | Secondary text, metadata |
| Line | `--line` | `#EBE7E2` | Hairlines, borders |
| **Persimmon** | `--accent` | `#C9502C` | Primary action, brand mark. The 10% |
| **Confirmed** | `--confirmed` | `#1F7A4D` | Checked items, verified prices — **semantic only** |

**Green is reserved and semantic.** It never decorates. It means *this is done* or *this
number is real* — the check circle, and a price you actually paid. That single rule links the
brand's color system to its credibility rule from §4.3: grey is a guess, green is a fact.

Category tints (produce `#E8F2E4`, dairy `#FDF0DC`, pantry `#ECEAF6`) are backgrounds for group
pills only — never text, never actions.

**Dark mode** is required, not optional; the app is used in dim stores and at night.
Base `#14130F`, card `#1F1E1B`, text `#F0EDE7`. Persimmon lightens to `#E4703F` to hold contrast.

All text pairs must clear **WCAG AA (4.5:1)**. High-contrast accessible palettes are a 2026
baseline expectation, and this app is read at arm's length in bad lighting.

### Typography

**UI: the system stack** — SF Pro on iOS, Roboto on Android. Not a compromise: it renders
faster, respects Dynamic Type, and inherits every accessibility affordance the OS ships. A
custom UI face would cost load time and accessibility for a font nobody notices while
shopping.

**Brand: one distinctive display face**, used only in marketing, store screenshots, onboarding
titles, and the wordmark. Bold, warm, slightly editorial — a humanist or transitional serif, or
a grotesque with real personality. This is where 2026's bold-typography trend belongs; not in
the list.

Scale in-app: 30/22/16/13.5/12.5 px, weights 400/550/650. Tabular numerals for every price.

### Iconography and item art

Emoji for item tiles at v1 — instant, free, universally legible, zero pipeline. Known limit:
Bring!'s icon-led UI degrades on long or unusual items, so **the text label is always primary
and the tile never carries meaning alone.** A missing tile must look intentional, not broken.

If the app earns a commissioned illustration set later, that's a v2 investment with real
ongoing cost. Don't confuse it with a launch requirement.

### App icon

Must survive 60×60 px on a crowded home screen next to green competitors.

- Persimmon field, single ink mark. **No text, no gradient, no photo.**
- The mark should read as a checked item or a small tally — legible at a glance, not literal.
- Test it greyscale, at 40 px, and beside Instacart, AnyList, and Apple Reminders. If it
  disappears in that row, it's wrong.
- The persimmon field alone does most of the differentiation work, since every neighbor is green.

### Motion

Sparing and physical. Check-off is the only animation that matters — it should feel like
setting something down. One completion moment per finished list. Nothing that delays a tap.
Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 6. Store presence

70% of downloads start with a store search, and the **top 3 results for a keyword take over
half its clicks**. Organic search is the only viable channel at this price point (`MARKET.md` §4),
so the listing *is* the marketing.

### Metadata

Title carries the brand plus head terms, subtitle carries the value proposition plus *new*
keywords, keyword field expands coverage. **Never repeat a keyword across the three** — it
wastes characters and adds no ranking value.

| Field | Value | Chars |
|---|---|---|
| Title | `Errand: Shared Grocery List` | 27 / 30 |
| Subtitle | `Aisle order & trip totals` | 25 / 30 |
| Keywords | `shopping,family,household,budget,price,pantry,checklist,offline,share,supermarket,no ads` | ≤100 |

Head terms ("grocery list," "shopping list") aren't winnable at launch. The long tail is, and it
maps onto the gaps: **shared grocery list**, **grocery list with prices**, **family shopping
list**, **grocery list no ads**, **grocery budget list**.

### Screenshots

Screenshot text is **OCR-indexed by Apple**, so captions are both conversion copy and ranking
signal. Strong screenshots are worth a 20–40% conversion lift. Caption first, UI second — users
give it about seven seconds and will not decode a raw screen.

Narrative order, one claim each:

1. **"Know what the trip costs."** — list with the running total
2. **"Sorted the way your store is."** — aisle-grouped list
3. **"Everyone's list, one list."** — household avatars and attribution
4. **"Works with no signal."** — offline state
5. **"No ads. Not now, not later."** — the positioning flag
6. **"Your prices, not a guess."** — price history / receipt scan (once it ships)

Change one variable at a time and test with Apple PPO or Play experiments — never ship a
simultaneous rewrite of title, subtitle, and screenshots, or you learn nothing.

## 7. Launch checklist

**Identity:** name cleared (§3.4) · wordmark · app icon at all sizes · color tokens in code ·
type scale · dark mode · one-page voice guide

**Store:** title/subtitle/keywords · 6 screenshots per device size · preview video (optional —
lower priority than screenshots) · description with the first three lines carrying the pitch ·
privacy nutrition labels (a differentiator here — fill them out honestly and prominently)

**Web:** landing page · privacy policy stating plainly that data isn't sold · support page

**Post-launch:** review-response templates in voice · a changelog written like a human ·
PPO test queued for screenshot order

## 8. Risks

1. **The name will probably not clear.** Short real-word marks in Class 9 are heavily
   contested. Screen five candidates in parallel and expect to lose two or three.
2. **"No ads" is only a moat while it's true.** It's the loudest claim in the brand, so
   reversing it later — even via "sponsored suggestions" — would be read as a betrayal, not a
   pivot. Make the promise only if the pricing in `MARKET.md` is one you'll actually hold to.
3. **Not-green is a bet.** It differentiates on a crowded shelf but forfeits instant "this is a
   grocery app" recognition. The subtitle and screenshots have to carry that job.
4. **Warmth ages into twee** without discipline. The line between "capable friend" and
   "cartoon vegetable" is enforced in code review of microcopy, not in a document.
