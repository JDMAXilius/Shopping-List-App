# Naming — expanded candidate pool

Supersedes `BRAND.md` §3. Every candidate below was checked against live App Store listings
via search. That is a **first-pass collision check, not a clearance** — see §5.

**Correction to `BRAND.md`: Errand is no longer recommended.** Evidence below.

---

## 1. Criteria (unchanged)

≤7 characters (top-100 average) · fits inside the 30-char store title alongside keywords ·
a real word used unexpectedly · spellable on first hearing, because word of mouth between
household members is the growth loop · not descriptive-generic · warm and adult, matching a
35–54 parent.

## 4. Shortlist

### Tier 1 — recommend screening these first

**1. Dozen** — *recommended*

Grocery-native without being literal: a dozen eggs is the most ordinary counting unit in a
supermarket. Ties to both pillars — counting items, and counting cost. Five characters, warm,
plain, impossible to misspell, easy to say across a kitchen. No shopping-list collision found.

*Snag:* it means twelve. A few people will ask "why twelve?" once, then stop. Minor, but real.

ASO title: `Dozen: Shared Grocery List` — 26 / 30

**2. Sundry** — *strong second*

The most precisely apt name in the pool. "Sundries" is a genuine retail term for miscellaneous
small goods, so the word already lives in this world without being a category cliché. Exactly
the "real word used unexpectedly" pattern that 2026 naming favours. No collision found.

*Snag:* a minority will hear "Sunday," and it's marginally harder to spell from speech than
Dozen — which matters when growth depends on one household member telling another.

ASO title: `Sundry: Shared Grocery List` — 27 / 30

**3. Docket** — *best literal meaning*

A docket *is* an itemized list of goods — and in the UK and Australia a delivery docket is
exactly that. Distinctive, six characters, nothing in the category.

*Snag:* US ears hear courtroom first. It's the coldest of the three against a brand
personality built on warmth, and that's a real mismatch rather than a quibble.

ASO title: `Docket: Shared Grocery List` — 27 / 30

### Tier 2 — viable, screen if Tier 1 fails

| Name | Case for it | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| **Chit** (4) | A chit is a short note or a running bill. Shortest in the pool, memorable, distinctive. | Obscure to many Americans; risks reading as a typo |
| **Ample** (5) | Warm, abundant, calm. Pairs beautifully with "enough" messaging. | No grocery link at all — the subtitle carries everything |
| **Reckon** (6) | Folksy and exact: to reckon is to total up. Very warm, very spoken. | Reads regional/Southern US to some; verb-as-name is slightly awkward |
| **Rounds** (6) | "Doing the rounds"; a milk round. Evokes the recurring weekly trip. | Plural names are weak in stores; medical connotation |
| **Bramble** (7) | Warm, hedgerow, berries. Genuinely charming and distinctive. | 7 chars, no direct grocery meaning, edges toward twee |
| **Icebox** (6) | Retro-American, warm, food-storage. | Points at the pantry feature we deprioritized |
| **Plenty** (6) | Excellent feeling — abundance without excess. | A vertical-farming company and a fintech both use it |

## 5. What this is not

Search-based App Store checking catches obvious collisions. It does **not** catch:

- **Registered marks with no app** — a trademark holder in Class 9 can block you without ever
  shipping software.
- **Common-law use** — an unregistered mark in active commercial use still blocks.
- **Phonetic near-misses** — USPTO refuses on sound-alikes, not just exact strings. *Dozen*
  vs *Dozzen*, *Sundry* vs *Sundri*.
- **Google Play** — I checked App Store listings only.
- **Non-US jurisdictions** — EUIPO and UKIPO are separate filings.

**Before any spend:** run all three Tier 1 names through USPTO **Class 9 and Class 42** in
parallel, including phonetic variants; check common-law use; check both stores; check `.com`
and `.app`; check handles. Then file. Expect to lose at least one — short real-word marks in
Class 9 are heavily contested, and screening three in parallel rather than sequentially is how
you avoid three months of dead time.

---

## 6. Recommendation

**Dozen**, with **Sundry** as the immediate fallback and **Docket** as third.

Dozen wins on the criterion that actually drives growth here: a household member has to say the
name out loud to another household member and have them find it. *Dozen* survives that.
*Sundry* mostly does. *Docket* does, but arrives cold.

If all three fail screening, Tier 2 is ranked as listed — start with **Chit** and **Ample**.

Note that the brand sheet in `design/brand-sheet.html` still renders "Errand" as a placeholder.
It needs re-rendering once a name clears; nothing else in the visual system depends on the name.
